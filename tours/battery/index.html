7 stations on this tour

  1. Congo — The Cobalt Mines

    The Democratic Republic of Congo produces 73% of the world's cobalt — an essential element in lithium-ion batteries. Much of it is mined by hand, including by an estimated 40,000 child miners in artisanal operations. A single smartphone battery contains 5-10g of cobalt.   2. Lithium Triangle — The White Gold

    Chile, Argentina, and Bolivia sit atop the "Lithium Triangle" — holding 58% of global lithium reserves. Lithium is extracted from salt flats (salars) by pumping mineral-rich brine into evaporation pools.   3. Australia — Hard Rock Lithium

    Australia is the world's largest lithium producer by volume, using hard-rock mining (spodumene) rather than brine extraction. The Greenbushes mine in Western Australia is the single largest lithium operation on Earth. Australia exports most raw material to China for processing — a dependency the government is trying to reverse with new…

  4. China — The Processing Monopoly

    China controls 60% of global lithium refining, 77% of battery cell manufacturing, and 80% of cobalt processing — even though it mines very little of either.   5. Taiwan — The Chip Bottleneck

    TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) fabricates over 90% of the world's most advanced chips — the processors in every phone, car, and AI server.   6. Gigafactories — The Assembly Line

    Tesla's Gigafactory Nevada produces more batteries annually than the entire world did in 2014. Similar megafactories are now rising across the U.S. (Georgia, Texas), Europe (Germany, Sweden, Hungary), and Asia.
