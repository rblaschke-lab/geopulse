6 stations on this tour

  1. MOUNT FUJI — JAPAN

    Japan sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire — a 40,000km horseshoe of seismic and volcanic activity. Mount Fuji (3,776m) last erupted in 1707 and is monitored 24/7. Japan experiences ~1,500 earthquakes per year.

  2. KRAKATOA & MERAPI — INDONESIA

    Indonesia has 130+ active volcanoes — the most of any country. Krakatoa's 1883 eruption was heard 5,000km away and caused a global temperature drop. The child volcano, Anak Krakatau, triggered a tsunami in 2018.

  3. CASCADES RANGE — USA

    The Cascade Range stretches from BC to California. Mount St. Helens' 1980 eruption killed 57 and was the deadliest in US history. Mount Rainier is considered the most dangerous due to lahar risk threatening 3M+ residents near Tacoma and Seattle.

  4. ANDES VOLCANOES — SOUTH AMERICA

    The Andes chain hosts Earth's highest active volcanoes. Cotopaxi (5,897m) in Ecuador is one of the world's most dangerous. Nevado del Ruiz in Colombia killed 23,000 people in 1985 when lahars buried the town of Armero.

  5. ICELAND — MID-ATLANTIC RIDGE

    Iceland sits directly on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where the Eurasian and North American tectonic plates pull apart. Eyjafjallajökull's 2010 eruption shut down European airspace for 6 days, stranding 10 million travelers. The island has 30+ active volcanic systems.

  6. THE COMPLETE RING OF FIRE

    The Pacific Ring of Fire accounts for 75% of all volcanic eruptions and 90% of all earthquakes worldwide. It stretches 40,000km from New Zealand, through Japan, across Alaska, and down the Americas. Over 450 volcanoes line this arc, making it the most geologically active zone on Earth.
